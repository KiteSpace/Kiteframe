Tokyo rewards planning a coffee morning as a route rather than a destination.
Three of the shops in this atlas sit close enough together to do in one go, and
because they disagree with each other about almost everything, the sequence
teaches you more than any of them would alone.

## 8:30 — Fuglen, Tomigaya

Start here because it opens early and because it is the most comfortable of the
three. A Norwegian coffee bar that emigrated to a Shibuya backstreet, kept its
mid-century furniture, and added a cocktail licence.

Order filter and sit down. The furniture is for sale, which means the room
changes slowly over months, and it is the only stop on this route where sitting
for an hour is clearly encouraged.

## 10:00 — Koffee Mameya, Omotesando

Twenty minutes on foot, and a complete change of premise.

There is no menu and nowhere to sit. You describe what you normally drink, and
someone behind the counter narrows a rotating selection of roasters down to
something for you. It is a consultation, and treating it as a quick stop misses
the point entirely.

The contrast with Fuglen is the useful part: one room is designed for you to
stay, the other for you to pay attention and leave.

## 11:30 — Glitch, Kanda-Nishikicho

A longer hop east into an office district that empties out at weekends.

Glitch roasts at the pale end of pale and pours nearly everything as filter.
Order two washed Ethiopians side by side — this is the stop where comparison is
easiest, because the room is quiet and the coffees are deliberately close
together.

## Why this order works

Comfortable, then instructive, then analytical. Reversed, the Glitch flight
would be wasted on a palate that has not warmed up, and Mameya's consultation
would land as an interrogation.

Weekday mornings only, if you have the choice. All three are small, and all
three are much better before the queues.
