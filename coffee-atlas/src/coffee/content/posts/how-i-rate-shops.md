Every entry in this atlas carries a rating out of five and a separate
recommended flag. People assume the flag is just "rating above four". It is not,
and the gap between them is the most useful thing in the dataset.

## What the rating measures

Execution, narrowly. Is the espresso pulled well and consistently? Is the batch
filter fresh rather than stewed? Does the room work for what it is trying to be?

That is deliberately a boring question, and it produces boring answers. Most
competent specialty bars land between 4 and 4.5. A 3.5 is not an insult; it
means something was uneven.

## What the recommendation means

Whether I would send someone out of their way for it.

Those come apart constantly:

- **Market Lane at Prahran Market** rates a straightforward 4. Carefully run,
  filter always on, nothing wrong with it. Not recommended, because there is no
  reason to cross Melbourne for it when Patricia is in the CBD.
- **St. John's in Covington** rates 3.5 and I nearly did recommend it. The
  coffee is ordinary. The porch, the ceiling fans, and the pace are not, and on
  a humid afternoon that is the entire product.

## Why keep both

Because a single number quietly collapses two different questions — "is this
done well" and "is this worth your morning" — and the collapse always favours
technical precision over atmosphere. Filtering by rating finds the reliably
good. Filtering by recommendation finds the memorable. The overlap is smaller
than the specialty coffee world likes to admit.

Both filters are in the toolbar. The recommendation one is the one I actually
use.
